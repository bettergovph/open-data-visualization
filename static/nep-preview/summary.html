Follow the changes between budget stages

NEP → HGAB (House version) → GAB (Senate version) → Bicam (House/Senate reconciliation) → GAA (enacted).

NEP and final GAA provide the published bookends. The investigative focus is how allocations change through House and Senate deliberations and bicameral reconciliation. A genuinely new HGAB line deserves scrutiny: who proposed it, what documented need supports it, and whether it remains in the Senate and reconciled versions. A lawful amendment can add projects; an unmatched parser row is not yet a demonstrated amendment.

These pages currently trace NEP and House readings. No survival claim is made for Senate GAB, Bicam or FY2027 GAA until those versions are available and reconciled. Retained NEP repeat candidates and new HGAB insertion candidates answer different questions.

Investigation priority 1 · Direct allocation observation; rainfall need remains a proxy

RCS: identical budget packages across unequal rainfall proxies

199 offices × ₱4.2M = ₱835.800M

Talking point
What policy or engineering design justifies one identical budget unit per office?
Observed evidence
All district-office RCS lines have the same amount. Province/regional rainfall proxies differ by 2.59×. Alternative formulas move different numbers of budget units. All 199 DEO lines are retained unchanged in HGAB; no differentiated needs weighting is visible in the amounts.
Plausible explanation
Standard package costs or a minimum coverage policy may justify equal allocations. Rainfall alone does not measure demand.
What investigators need next
Obtain siting, design capacity, water demand, catchment area and existing RCS inventory.
Parsing/location caution
DEO is not LEG; province rainfall is not a district observation. Returning FY2026 program.

Length and chainage gaps limit budget scrutiny

Roads: 51.14% · Bridges: 94.74% · FMR: 99.75% without usable extracted length

Can the funded segment and engineering length be established for every allocation?
Observed evidence
Roads: 3,175/6,498 lines have derivable stated lengths · Bridges: 60/1,140 lines have derivable stated lengths · FMR: 2/795 lines have derivable stated lengths. Official programs have separate denominators on the supporting page.
Plausible explanation
Lengths may be recorded in engineering schedules or continuation text not recovered by this extraction; incomplete metadata need not reflect deliberate omission.

Missing includes absent and unusable extracted evidence, not proof the original documents lack length. Coordinate distances do not establish construction length; mixed scopes are separate.

Investigation priority 8 · Descriptive allocation/segment-length proxy; comparable engineering costs unavailable

Allocation per stated meter: inspect scope before comparing rates

Roads: 3,175 screening ratios · Bridges: 60 screening ratios · FMR: 2 screening ratios

Talking point
Do apparently high rates reflect short printed segments, structures, broader funded scope, or unusually costly comparable work?
Observed evidence
Whole HGAB line-item allocation is divided by its derivable stated segment meters. Roads, bridges and FMR are displayed separately, with official work-program filters. Mixed road/bridge lines receive no single-work rate.
Plausible explanation
Terrain, width, structures, approaches, rehabilitation scope and components outside the stated segment can explain large differences. Tiny printed ranges or parser errors can produce extreme ratios.
What investigators need next
Verify station endpoints on original pages, then compare quantities, width, funded extent, designs and bill-of-quantity costs within the same work type. Investigate unusually short denominators before interpreting extreme ratios.
Parsing/location caution
These are appropriations per stated segment meter, not verified construction unit costs. Bridge chainage is not necessarily bridge span. No cross-category average, overpricing estimate or corruption probability is established.

Parsing and location gotchas

NEP → HGAB (House) → GAB (Senate) → Bicam → GAA are different stages; prior-year GAA is enacted, current proposals are not. Match allocation records at the same hierarchy before comparing money.

  • Printed-total reconciliation catches amount/hierarchy errors but cannot certify title identity or location.
  • Preserve source pages and occurrence counts. One comparison row may group multiple allocations; headers are not projects.
  • Require barangay → town/city → province context. DEO numbers do not identify legislative districts.
  • Keep directional names, tranches, province splits and construction chainage distinct. The confirmed multi-place Butuan/RTR line stays outside strict LEG totals.
  • Strict congressional rankings cover only 37.7% of extracted allocation value. Names indicate geography, not authorship or sponsorship.

How the ranking is calculated

  1. Potential insertion: the current matcher identifies no plausible NEP counterpart; ambiguous links and FAPs are excluded from this flag.
  2. Repeated allocation amount: the same exact positive amount occurs in the same PAP at least 10 times across at least 3 named implementing offices. This includes amounts such as ₱5M when the group qualifies; roundness alone adds no point.
  3. Historical title recurrence: a FY2025 or FY2026 GAA title agrees after case/punctuation normalization and removal of complete trailing station/coordinate metadata. A matching qualified municipality and province is required. Work descriptions, directions, packages and phases remain. Conflicting locations/tranches and disjoint stated chainages are excluded.
  4. Historical chainage overlap: an accepted historical counterpart has explicit, positive-length overlap of stated construction ranges. This adds one scope indicator, not proof of duplicate paid work.

Each type contributes one point; multiple historical years do not increase the score. Historical recurrence and chainage overlap are related signals, so the count is not a statistical probability or independent-evidence score. Exact ranking ties use the source occurrence ID. Missing historical links can reflect conservative matching, incomplete local coverage or unresolved names.

Standard packages, phased work and legitimate amendments can produce these signals. Names, roles and party affiliations add no points.
