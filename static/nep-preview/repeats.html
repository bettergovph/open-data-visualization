Coordinate and needs-screening methodology

Haversine great-circle separation, radius 6,371,008.8 meters, between one unambiguous decimal point per title. Latitude/longitude order inferred only from nonoverlapping local screening ranges (4–22 latitude, 116–127 longitude), recorded per point. At least four decimal places required. No geocoding, centroid substitution, nearest-coordinate join or coordinate borrowing from other rows/stages.

  • Printed points are not verified building footprints. Identical or nearby coordinates can be rounded, copied or reference a barangay center. Decimal precision is not positional accuracy.
  • Under-50-meter pairs are interpreted as likely same-site references for scope review. They are not counted as two confirmed buildings. Site identity requires maps, asset IDs, designs or field verification; nearby plots can contain different structures.
  • Coordinates pass a broad Philippines numeric-range check, not a barangay/province boundary-containment check. Large separation under matching labels may reflect coordinate or location errors.
  • A same-barangay identity requires agreement at barangay, municipality and province levels. Matching city wording without a qualified hierarchy is separately marked unverified.
  • One MPB per barangay is a needs-screening hypothesis, not a validated entitlement rule. Different functions, population, capacity, accessibility and hazard exposure may justify multiple facilities.
  • Historical completion/repair allocations can fund the same legitimate building in successive years. A short distance does not establish two separate buildings or duplicate payment.
  • Farther points can support a separate-site interpretation but do not establish need. The 50/100/250/1000-meter bins are descriptive review bands, not corruption thresholds.
  • Pairs are not distinct projects; a cluster of three items yields three pairs. Do not sum pair allocations as losses.

Methodology and exclusions

Compare distinct allocation occurrence IDs within each stage only. FAPs and untrusted NEP primary titles excluded. Same normalized official PAP and either qualified municipality/province or same implementing office required. At least three shared distinctive nonnumeric title tokens required. Same base title after trailing geographic metadata removal, or ≥0.94 SequenceMatcher similarity with qualified municipality/province AND identical distinctive token sets, produces a candidate. Changed route-endpoint or other distinctive scope tokens veto even when text similarity is high. Different explicit bridge numbers identify different structures and veto a pair, even on the same bypass road. Direction, qualified-location and tranche conflicts veto. Explicit disjoint ranges veto. Printed range endpoints are assumed to be ordered min/max. Mixed bare/plus ranges retain an unknown endpoint: a known maximum at or below another range’s known minimum excludes positive overlap, without resolving length. Other mixed ranges remain incomplete; possible overlap is not certified overlap. No amount-equality requirement. Missing chainage never proves scope overlap.

  • Matching allocation occurrences does not establish duplicate contracts, duplicate payment, fraud or authorship. Different engineering components and legitimate amendments can share titles.
  • Office-only agreement is weaker than site evidence. Unresolved locations are never inferred from similar names.
  • Pair counts are not project counts: three linked records produce three pairs. Distinct occurrence counts are shown separately; amounts are not summed as loss.
  • Exact official PAP blocking, trusted-title requirements and conservative location rules miss possible cross-program, paraphrased or unresolved duplicates. No flag is not clearance.
  • Tranche differences are excluded from this conservative queue, not certified as legitimate. Missing chainage requires source and engineering-scope review.
  • NEP baseline includes source records outside the API. Its documented source-title confidence limits remain applicable.
