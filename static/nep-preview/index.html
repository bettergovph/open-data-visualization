Follow the changes between budget stages

NEP → HGAB (House version) → GAB (Senate version) → Bicam (House/Senate reconciliation) → GAA (enacted).

NEP and final GAA provide the published bookends. The investigative focus is how allocations change through House and Senate deliberations and bicameral reconciliation. A genuinely new HGAB line deserves scrutiny: who proposed it, what documented need supports it, and whether it remains in the Senate and reconciled versions. A lawful amendment can add projects; an unmatched parser row is not yet a demonstrated amendment.

These pages currently trace NEP and House readings. No survival claim is made for Senate GAB, Bicam or FY2027 GAA until those versions are available and reconciled. Retained NEP repeat candidates and new HGAB insertion candidates answer different questions.

Investigation priority 1 · Direct allocation observation; rainfall need remains a proxy

RCS: identical budget packages across unequal rainfall proxies

199 offices × ₱4.2M = ₱835.800M

Talking point
What policy or engineering design justifies one identical budget unit per office?
Observed evidence
All district-office RCS lines have the same amount. Province/regional rainfall proxies differ by 2.59×. Alternative formulas move different numbers of budget units. All 199 DEO lines are retained unchanged in HGAB; no differentiated needs weighting is visible in the amounts.
Plausible explanation
Standard package costs or a minimum coverage policy may justify equal allocations. Rainfall alone does not measure demand.
What investigators need next
Obtain siting, design capacity, water demand, catchment area and existing RCS inventory.
Parsing/location caution
DEO is not LEG; province rainfall is not a district observation. Returning FY2026 program.

Roads: 3,329/6,482 lines have derivable stated lengths · Bridges: 65/1,159 lines have derivable stated lengths · FMR: 3/964 lines have derivable stated lengths. Official programs have separate denominators on the supporting page.

Investigation priority 8 · Descriptive allocation/segment-length proxy; comparable engineering costs unavailable

Allocation per stated meter: inspect scope before comparing rates

189 road and 2 bridge high-rate candidates · FMR peers insufficient

Talking point
Do apparently high rates reflect short printed segments, structures, broader funded scope, or unusually costly comparable work?
Observed evidence
Whole HGAB line-item allocation is divided by its derivable stated segment meters. Roads, bridges and FMR are displayed separately, with official work-program filters. Mixed road/bridge lines receive no single-work rate. Data-derived length groups and within-program slope-change screens are provided; no fixed 100-meter boundary is imposed. Lane evidence now supports 3 title-stated lane rates, 279 stated-length inventory lane estimates, and 390 existing-bridge asset lane estimates. These bases are shown separately and are not pooled into the per-meter flags. Recorded-condition screening identifies 2 rehabilitation lines on fully matched Good/Fair assessed road segments and 0 pavement-work lines within the disclosed five-budget-year surfacing window. These are review leads; later deterioration and different work scope may justify the allocations.
Plausible explanation
Terrain, width, structures, approaches, rehabilitation scope and components outside the stated segment can explain large differences. Tiny printed ranges or parser errors can produce extreme ratios.
What investigators need next
Verify station endpoints on original pages, then compare quantities, width, funded extent, designs and bill-of-quantity costs within the same work type. Investigate unusually short denominators before interpreting extreme ratios.
Parsing/location caution
These are appropriations per stated segment meter, not verified construction unit costs. Bridge chainage is not necessarily bridge span. No cross-category average, overpricing estimate or corruption probability is established. Inventory lanes are existing asset characteristics, not verified funded quantities; widening needs additional funded lanes. Overpasses, flyovers and viaducts use bridge peers.

Investigation priority 9 · Original PDF text agreement; funded engineering extent unresolved

Very short printed road ranges need engineering clarification

3 checked road ranges imply less than 5 meters

Talking point
Do the printed station endpoints correctly describe the funded segment, or do notation and scope need correction?
Observed evidence
Original-page visible text agrees with sampled road titles whose station differences calculate to 0.587 meters, 1.49 meters, 4.67 meters. Across all 3,175 known road lengths, only 204 (6.43%) are below 100 meters; high-rate sorting concentrates short denominators at the top.
Plausible explanation
A source-document typo, station convention, short component within a larger project, or broader funded scope can explain these values. A very short printed range does not establish the physical length actually funded.
What investigators need next
Request corrected or certified station endpoints, funded-segment plans, engineering quantities and allocation scope; verify notation before interpreting extreme allocation-per-meter rates.
Parsing/location caution
This is a printed-range/denominator issue, not evidence that most road projects are short or overpriced. Sampled title agreement rules out a title-transcription mismatch for those examples, not source errors or engineering ambiguity. Bridge lengths require separate interpretation.

Parsing and location gotchas

NEP → HGAB (House) → GAB (Senate) → Bicam → GAA are different stages; prior-year GAA is enacted, current proposals are not. Match allocation records at the same hierarchy before comparing money.

  • Printed-total reconciliation catches amount/hierarchy errors but cannot certify title identity or location.
  • Preserve source pages and occurrence counts. One comparison row may group multiple allocations; headers are not projects.
  • Require barangay → town/city → province context. DEO numbers do not identify legislative districts.
  • Keep directional names, tranches, province splits and construction chainage distinct. The confirmed multi-place Butuan/RTR line stays outside strict LEG totals.
  • Strict congressional rankings cover only 38.6% of extracted allocation value. Names indicate geography, not authorship or sponsorship.

How the ranking is calculated

  1. Potential insertion: the current matcher identifies no plausible NEP counterpart; ambiguous links and FAPs are excluded from this flag.
  2. Repeated allocation amount: the same exact positive amount occurs in the same PAP at least 10 times across at least 3 named implementing offices. This includes amounts such as ₱5M when the group qualifies; roundness alone adds no point.
  3. Historical title recurrence: a FY2025 or FY2026 GAA title agrees after case/punctuation normalization and removal of complete trailing station/coordinate metadata. A matching qualified municipality and province is required. Work descriptions, directions, packages and phases remain. Conflicting locations/tranches and disjoint stated chainages are excluded.
  4. Historical chainage overlap: an accepted historical counterpart has explicit, positive-length overlap of stated construction ranges. This adds one scope indicator, not proof of duplicate paid work.

Each type contributes one point; multiple historical years do not increase the score. Historical recurrence and chainage overlap are related signals, so the count is not a statistical probability or independent-evidence score. Exact ranking ties use the source occurrence ID. Missing historical links can reflect conservative matching, incomplete local coverage or unresolved names.

Standard packages, phased work and legitimate amendments can produce these signals. Names, roles and party affiliations add no points. Input hashes and all ranked rows are available in the full ranking JSON. The CSV exports all 25 displayed rows with their evidence. Amounts overlap the findings above; do not add them as alleged losses.

Condition and prior-surfacing flags

One point for rehabilitation/reconstruction/major repair when the full matched road range was assessed Good/Fair in a known pre-budget year. One point for pavement work when the full matched range has a positive recorded last-surfacing year within five budget years. Widening, approaches, slope/drainage and off-carriageway scope are excluded. Unknowns and partial coverage add no point. The five-year window is a screening choice, not an engineering service-life standard. Bridge Good/Fair context without an assessment date remains a review lead and earns no dated-condition point. Later deterioration or different funded components may justify the work. Condition, recent surfacing and historical recurrence can be related; points are not independent evidence or probabilities.

New cost-per-meter flag

One additional point when allocation per stated meter exceeds both twice the peer median and Q3 + 1.5 × IQR. Peers share the work category, structure group, matched surface/condition/assessment-year context, exact official PAP and a data-derived length band, with at least 20 records whose stated lengths are at least 10 meters. No fixed 100-meter cutoff is used. Extreme rates remain in this review table with a caution but are excluded from rate charts. Missing lengths, sub-10-meter printed ranges and insufficient peers add no point. This flag does not establish overpricing: width, terrain, structures, quantities and funded extent remain unverified. See full rate and grouping methodology.
