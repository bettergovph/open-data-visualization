A mixed signal

Different POWs are a positive sign; the identical allocation still needs explanation.

The extracted POWs show meaningful variation in stated facilities, quantities, and bill items—for example, Davao City examples name six versus fourteen school sites. That is evidence the documents are not simply identical copy-paste packages, and is a positive sign of project-specific preparation.

At the same time, all 199 district-office projects receive exactly ₱4.2M despite those differences. This does not show that any estimate is wrong, but it raises a separate question: do these varied scopes independently cost to the same amount, or is ₱4.2M acting as a standard ceiling/package? The signed estimates, quantities, standard design, and any allocation policy would help resolve that.

Do RCS POWs identify water storage?

Rainwater collection needs a way to retain collected water. A tank/cistern/reservoir line is evidence that storage is named, but it does not verify capacity, connection to gutters, or completed installation. Conversely, no matching term in extracted text does not prove the design omits storage: the relevant detail may be in drawings, specifications, an unparsed page, or under another description.

Raw Proposal POW scan: 25 of 199 RCS project PDFs were available in the local on-demand cache and scanned across all pages, including OCR for image-only pages. 12 of those 25 explicitly mention a tank, cistern, or reservoir. The other 174 projects had no raw Proposal POW PDF in that local cache; this does not establish whether their PDFs exist elsewhere on T5.
