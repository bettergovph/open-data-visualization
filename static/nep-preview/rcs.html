Uniform allocations · formula sensitivity

All 199 district-office lines receive one ₱4.2M budget unit, while their rainfall proxies span 1,564–4,045 mm/year (2.59×). Allocation coefficient of variation is 0%; rainfall-proxy variation is 19.1%. This establishes uniformity despite variable rainfall; it does not establish that planners ignored every factor.

Units moved = half the sum of absolute changes; all scenarios conserve 199 budget units. Largest-remainder rounding uses office name to break ties. A budget unit is not a verified count of installed collectors. Province means are shared proxies, not 199 independent rainfall observations.

Hypothesis for investigators: the flat amount is a standard package rather than a needs-based allocation. Alternatives include a minimum-coverage policy or common construction specification. Pearson correlation is undefined because every allocation is identical; a p-value against an invented random-allocation process would be misleading. Neither rainfall formula is a recommended final budget, and a model assigning zero units is not evidence an office needs none.

Mean versus median weighting changes whole-unit recommendations for 0 offices. 1 office uses a regional rainfall fallback. Establish the actual site/catchment area, design capacity, water demand, storage, access and existing assets before making a needs claim.

The direct per-office rainfall formula rounds to one whole budget unit for every office, even though fractional shares differ. Equal whole units therefore do not, by themselves, refute rainfall weighting at this budget-unit resolution. The existing province-first scenario moves 44 units because provinces receive a share before it is divided among offices. That structural choice must be justified before claiming it is a better allocation.
