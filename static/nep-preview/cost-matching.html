How much allocation per lane-meter?

One lane-meter is one meter of one lane. A two-lane, 100-meter funded segment has 200 lane-meters. Only explicit title lane counts supply the title-stated measure below. Existing inventory lanes supply a separate estimate; they do not verify the lanes funded by the allocation. Widening needs the additional lanes actually funded.

Dimensions recovered from matched assets

Existing asset dimensions can be recovered even when a title does not state the funded length. Road figures describe the recorded contiguous extent of the matched inventory section, which may not cover the whole section; bridge figures describe the existing bridge. They are separate from funded-project length coverage and do not fill missing project lengths or enter per-meter price flags. Counts are budget line items, so an asset may occur more than once.

Lane matching and exclusions

  • Title lane-meter rate = whole allocation / (usable stated meters × explicit title lane count). It is not verified construction cost.
  • Inventory proxy = whole allocation / (stated meters × inventory lanes). Road lane counts are measure-weighted when counts vary along fully covered ranges.
  • Existing-asset dimensions are recovered separately from funded project length: bridge inventory span/width/lanes, or the union of road section intervals with measure-weighted lanes/widths. Gaps and conflicting or unknown measurements are withheld. Section size never fills a missing funded segment.
  • Only exact printed section/bridge IDs or a complete exact road name with compatible region/DEO and unique section qualify; no fuzzy names.
  • Kilometer post brackets map global K station values to section-local measure by interpolation, never extrapolation. Ambiguous or conflicting references are withheld.
  • Widening needs funded additional lanes; approaches, slope/drainage and mixed scopes are withheld. Bridge span mismatch above 10% prevents the proxy.
  • Separate existing-bridge asset estimate = allocation / (inventory bridge span × inventory lanes), only for rehabilitation/major repair or retrofitting/strengthening. This is allocation intensity over the existing asset, not a funded-length rate; it never fills stated length or enters high-rate flags. Replacement, new construction and widening are excluded.
  • Inventory copyright is preserved. Old inventory does not establish the lane count at the FY2027 funded segment.
  • Inventory proxies remain separate from title-stated rates, historical comparisons and per-meter high-rate flags. No missing funded lane quantity is invented.

Surface, condition and prior surfacing

Condition flag: rehabilitation/reconstruction/major repair only, full matched road range assessed Good/Fair with a known pre-budget assessment year. Recent-surfacing flag: rehabilitation, preventive maintenance or other explicitly described pavement work where the full matched range has a positive recorded last-surfacing year within five budget years; unknown/zero values are not filled. Widening, approaches, slope/drainage and off-carriageway scopes excluded. Bridge condition is displayed, but an unknown assessment year prevents the dated flag.

Review leads, not proof of waste or incorrect prioritization. Assessment and budget dates differ; later deterioration, structural/safety work or different funded components may justify rehabilitation. Last-surfacing inventory is older and cannot establish that no later work occurred.

Length extraction and coverage methodology

  • Complete STA/Station/K/KM plus-form ranges are converted to meters. Explicit “Chainage 287 - Chainage 1287” values are meter offsets, giving a 1,000-meter range. Mixed bare/plus notation, such as Chainage 200 to Chainage 3 + 500, is incomplete: the bare endpoint may lack a kilometer component. No length or rate is calculated until its units and endpoint are verified. For repeat screening only, printed endpoints are assumed to be ordered min/max: a known maximum at another segment’s known minimum excludes positive overlap even if length remains uncertain. Overlapping numerical intervals are merged, never double-counted. Multiple named routes with unverified station references are withheld.
  • A single explicitly labeled length in meters or kilometers can supply a length. Widths, isolated stations and coordinate straight-line distances do not supply a road length.
  • FMR length extraction uses only the current project's row-bounded source context, including printed STA labels in the analyst's coordinate cell. Context preceding the current project title is discarded, preventing a previous project’s endpoint from becoming part of the current length. Exactly two printed STA endpoints in the remaining context can form a range. The context is retained in the downloadable data; nearby unrelated text is not searched for endpoints.
  • Partial or zero-length chainage, conflicting explicit lengths and ambiguous multi-route scopes remain without a usable length. No allocation amount is used to infer missing length.
  • The missing percentage is (all category occurrences minus occurrences with usable stated length) / all category occurrences. It includes both absent metadata and metadata that needs review. A separate rate count distinguishes usable lengths from permissible single-category ratios.
  • Mixed road/bridge titles are separated and no single-work rate is shown. Flood, building/water PAPs and foreign-assisted schedules are outside this page's denominator. Classification uses explicit titles and PAPs; it is not a complete inventory of physical road assets.
  • No rate averages are pooled across categories or PAPs. Every point identifies one allocation occurrence; rankings can change with scope, width, structures, terrain, total project components and parser corrections. High rates warrant source/quantity review, not an allegation.
  • Graphs and CSV follow table filters. Each category section shows one pooled box plot for its projects with usable stated-meter rates. The official-program filter can narrow the plotted projects; every point remains visible and outliers are marked, not removed. Segments under 10m and rows without usable length remain in the table/CSV but are not plotted. Source volume/page and original length evidence are included. Historical stages are not used to fill current-stage gaps.

The summary links to two separate leads: length-data coverage and allocation per stated meter. Coverage gaps are extraction observations, and ratios are scope-dependent screening proxies. Extremely short printed ranges can produce extreme rates; verify station endpoints and funded scope on original pages before interpreting them. Request bills of quantities, designs, funded segment boundaries, bridge spans and roadway widths before comparing actual costs.
