1. Stated-meter rates

Which projects have unusually high allocation per meter?

The first tables compare each rate with the typical rate in the same official program and a similar stated-length group. A high-rate candidate is more than twice the typical peer rate and unusually high within its peer group. This is a scope-review queue, not a verified overpricing finding. Extreme values remain visible as individual points in rate charts. Printed segments under 10m remain in tables and CSV but are excluded from rate charts. Coverage denominators include all selected extracted occurrences in each category, including those without a usable length.

These are whole line-item allocations divided by stated segment lengths, not verified construction unit costs. Bridge chainage may cover approaches rather than bridge span. Missing percentage means no usable length was recovered from the current title/project context; it does not establish that the original budget or engineering documents omit length.

Outliers remain visible in rate charts as individual points. Printed segments under 10m remain in tables and CSV but are not plotted. Peer screening needs at least 20 usable records. Missing or sparse peers do not establish a normal price.

How high-rate candidates are identified

Peers share category, structure group (overpasses/flyovers, viaducts and pedestrian structures separated), official PAP, a data-derived length band, and matched surface/condition/assessment-year context. Unknown context is pooled only with unknown context and remains explicitly unadjusted. At least 20 peers with stated length ≥10m. Candidate rate exceeds both 2× peer median and Q3+1.5×IQR. Chart extremes exceed max(2×median,Q3+3×IQR); sub-10m printed ranges also stay off rate charts. Lane inventory estimates do not enter these per-meter flags.

2. Lane-meter analysis

Lane-meter calculations divide allocation by meters × lanes. Title-stated lanes, existing-inventory lane estimates and whole-bridge asset estimates are displayed separately. See matching rules, exclusions and source vintage. Existing asset size does not fill a missing funded length.

The segmented model fits log total allocation against log stated length within each PAP. It needs 80 records and 20 per side, excludes lengths below 10 meters, and requires BIC improvement ≥6 over a single slope. The boundary is selected from observed-length candidates, not a fixed 100-meter threshold; bootstrap frequency describes how consistently it is selected. The 10th–90th percentile of bootstrap-selected boundaries describes an empirical stability window, not a confidence interval. The ≥25-meter sensitivity boundary is also shown.

Method and limitations

Length bands are derived separately within each PAP using approximately equal-count groups (up to eight, targeting at least 20 records per group). Equal observed lengths stay together. Breakpoint candidates are geometric midpoints between adjacent observed distinct lengths, requiring at least 20 records on either side; up to 31 candidates cover the eligible boundaries. No 100-meter boundary is prescribed. The searched boundary counts as an extra model parameter in BIC.

  • Density=allocation/length; declining density can follow mechanically from a fixed budget.
  • Quantile-band counts and 2×/BIC screens are editorial exploratory choices, not calibrated corruption thresholds.
  • Models use total allocation rather than density to reduce the arithmetic-denominator trap.
  • Bootstrap votes measure selection stability in this sample, not the probability of a real engineering threshold.
  • Dependence between allocation occurrences, unknown width/terrain/structures and non-random missing lengths remain uncontrolled.
  • Sub-10m rows stay visible in bands but are excluded from model fits; ≥25m sensitivity is reported.
  • Multiple exploratory program/boundary searches are not confirmatory significance tests; no p-values or multiplicity-adjusted claims are made.
  • Sparse bridge/FMR data cannot establish an engineering threshold. No pooled cross-category rate model.

Download all groups, model slopes, bootstrap votes and methodology. Patterns direct source and engineering review; there is no universal safe or suspicious length.

4. Coordinate-distance approximations

Coordinate-distance estimates stay separate from stated-meter rates and high-rate flags. Detailed asset matching, recovered dimensions, length coverage and extraction methods are on Asset matching and length coverage.

Coordinate-based allocation per meter · approximation

Additional tables below divide whole allocations by straight-line separation between two printed endpoints. Separately labeled FMR segment chords are summed. A road can curve or include structures, so this distance is not a measured road or bridge length. These approximate rates are kept outside stated-length coverage, historical price comparisons and high-rate flags. Review flags, coincident endpoints, unpaired coordinate lists and mixed road/bridge rates are withheld. No distance between different projects supplies a project-length denominator.
