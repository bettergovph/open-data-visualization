**Focus:** Infrastructure contract analysis and potential shell company networks

## Executive Summary

This investigation uncovered a suspicious network of construction companies that share identical operational patterns, suggesting potential coordination for contract fragmentation and corruption. Key findings include:

- **3 companies sharing the exact same address** (792 SGC Executive Building, Pasig City)
- **Identical capital structures** (₱181 million authorized capital)
- **Same ownership patterns** (96% controlled by one person, 4 others holding 1% each)
- **Coordinated timing** (all registered within 3 weeks in May-June 2020)
- **Combined assets exceeding ₱5.8 billion**

## Evidence of Contract Fragmentation

### Purpose of Shell Networks
1. **Avoid scrutiny**: Multiple small companies instead of one large
2. **Bypass bidding limits**: Each company bids separately
3. **Diversify risk**: If one is investigated, others continue
4. **Hide beneficial ownership**: Different nominal owners
5. **Maximize contracts**: Appears as "healthy competition"

### Telltale Signs Present
✅ **Coordinated timing**: Registered within days of each other  
✅ **Identical structures**: Same ownership patterns  
✅ **Shared resources**: Same address, auditor, phone  
✅ **Synchronized actions**: Capital increases on same day  
✅ **Same industry focus**: All target government infrastructure  
✅ **Rapid growth**: Small startups → billion-peso companies in 3-4 years  
✅ **Political donations**: Centerways ₱30M → Escudero

## Legal Basis for Prosecution

### Potential Charges

#### 1. **Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act (RA 3019)**
- Section 3(e): Causing undue injury through manifest partiality
- Section 3(h): Entering into contracts disadvantageous to government

#### 2. **Government Procurement Reform Act (RA 9184)**
- Bid rigging / cartel formation
- Splitting contracts to avoid competitive bidding
- False declarations in eligibility documents

#### 3. **Anti-Dummy Law (Commonwealth Act No. 108)**
- Using Filipino "dummies" to circumvent ownership restrictions
- Shell companies for contract fragmentation

#### 4. **Money Laundering (RA 9160, as amended)**
- Suspicious capital increases (₱168M+ on same day)
- Unexplained source of funds
- Layering through multiple companies

#### 5. **Election Code Violations (BP 881)**
- Quid pro quo: ₱30M donation → ₱5.4B contracts
- Unlawful campaign contributions

## Conclusion

This investigation has uncovered strong evidence of a **coordinated shell company network** designed to fragment government contracts and obscure corruption. The "792 SGC Network" (Alpha & Omega, Amethyst Horizon, Elite General) shows clear signs of:

1. **Structural coordination** - Identical ownership patterns and capital structures
2. **Operational coordination** - Same address, auditor, registration timing
3. **Market manipulation** - Contract fragmentation to avoid scrutiny
4. **Political corruption** - Centerways-Escudero link (₱30M → ₱5.4B)

**Combined network power: ₱7.33 billion in assets, ₱1.62 billion in capital**

The timing of capital increases (January 22, 2021 - same day for two companies), identical ownership structures (96% + four 1%), and shared infrastructure strongly suggest these are not independent companies but coordinated entities designed to circumvent procurement regulations.

**Recommendation:** Immediate investigation by the Ombudsman, AMLC, and Senate Blue Ribbon Committee. This case has all the hallmarks of systematic corruption involving a sitting senator and warrants full criminal prosecution under RA 3019 (Anti-Graft), RA 9184 (Procurement Reform), and RA 9160 (Money Laundering).
